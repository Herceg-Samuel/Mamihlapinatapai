Let me begin by saying this, I do not know. And I dare say, we do not either, and cannot altogether!

I also hold that we cannot define love, we do not have the tools for such an endeavor.

There are different kinds of love, especially in other languages. In English, the separation has been tried; by use of terms like platonic love, romantic love and the rest.

Maybe this is the weakness of this inherited language. The Englishman suffers as he tries to explain his love, and in the many words, he says nothing!
He loses the one he supposedly loves.

In Greek, we have

1. philia, where we get philosophy, meaning love of wisdom(Sophia),
2. eros, the romantic kind
3. agape, the God love, sacrificial
4. storge, parental or family love
5. pragma, where we get pragmatic(practical)
6. philautia, self-love
7. mania, obsessive or possessive(maniac, reminds me of Conan)

In the Symposium, they try to answer this question(I am yet to finish reading it)

I believe we cannot properly define things as they are, we mostly attribute properties
or functions.

As I've heard Alex O'Connor say time and again, concerning the scientific; science cannot tell
us what this are, it only describes what they do. For example,
Newton can only describe what gravity does; causes things to fall,
the moon to rotate the earth etc., but never what gravity is.
To the science man, this is an absurd question. If we are to push
things further, what are things made out of? Matter? What is that
made of? Even if we get to subatomic level, there is still a thing
of lack, we cannot, at least by the scientific achieve this.
This in itself is what is described as a category error!

In the same manner, we can talk about the properties associated
with love; patience, suffering, joy, kindness and what love does;
making men mad, arising of all sorts of feelings,, among all that you already know!

My case as will be further discussed is; objects or things as we think of them do not exist. We have ideas of them but never the actual thing.(More on this soon)

This is synonymous with infinity, we think of it as a thing, at
least others do, and we always approach it but never get to it.
We have this overall conception and idea of love, based on certain things.
We can't put our fingers on, let alone get a hold of it.
