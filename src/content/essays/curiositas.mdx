## Step back

A few days ago(then), I shared some stuff about curiosity(WhatsApp status).
I Was fired up. Really fired up.

I think this is because curiosity is the closest thing to my heart,
it has been the guide in this light lacking world and mostly on the
intricate and confusing matters of the "self". Getting my guide mutilated,
killed and destroyed would not be entirely fun or pleasing, And maybe, just maybe,
I instinctively wrote an "apology"!(well, not really)

One of them: curiosity killed the cat. It's actually care killed the cat.
(One of the stuff I'm collecting for my classic lost in translation )

## Backstory

Most of it was based on something I learnt from The Rest is Science on YT. Video on curiosity from Hannah Fry and Michael Stevens(Vsauce).

[Check them out](https://www.youtube.com/@TheRestIsScience/videos)

From them I learnt that there was a time curiosity was considered a vice. Mostly a mention from St. Thomas Aquinas.(It has been clarified
by Hannah as well that curiosity then was not as we know it today, or at least how it was use. It was associated with "taking the wrong route",
in rejection of the right, pride the epicenter)

Then on Saturday I believe, I wrote about it. Read more on it.

Curiositas. This has been in my head for some time. Curiosity sat at the same table as pride and gluttony. Wow!

Today, I asked myself, what is it that he knew then that made him make that claim.

I mean everyone makes claims based on certain elements, even crazy people, let alone a scholar.
And given the scholarly excellence acquitted to him, careful treading and understanding are to be sought!(
This is by no means a wave of hand appeal to authority
)

I have yet learnt of what he considered(To the deepest detail). However, I have learnt to live with even more wonder.

Steven Furtick mentioned something. We can be impressive or a blessing(In relation to what we want to achieve, in our lives and that of others).

Now I am left wondering. What seat do I occupy?
We got a job to do(He added).

I do not know where this goes but it is better to consider the best case scenario when it comes to people.
Giving what they call the benefit of doubt, mostly in alignment with someone's character, as we know them.

In computation, the worst case scenario is really handy. It gives a proper estimation of time and cost to be taken and
lets us brace ourselves to the provision of the said resources!
However, people are more than numbers(as I have alluded to several times).

It's not to say that we assume intentions. It's to live knowing they had a perspective.
It is left to be right or wrong. But we cannot dispute of its existence.

Obviously, people can be wrong, have been wrong and will continue being wrong. I may be chronically wrong.

The enemy of certainty is certainty.

Again, do unto others what you'd want done unto you.

We'll get to the good curiosity deep dive.

Stay curious!(even if it seems defiant, and perhaps, especially then!)
