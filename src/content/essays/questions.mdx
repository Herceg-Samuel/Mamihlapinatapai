Given that questions are birthed out of curiosity
and we have in some capacity come to the
conclusion that curiosity is in itself good
and sometimes necessary in the lives of
rational beings, will it be safe to conclude
the same for questions? Does the child come
with the mother or are we to examine
the child in all many ways, to determine
its "usefulness" and "necessity",
with the same if not more scrutiny as the
mother to the production of a clear response with regards to perpetuation of its life?

# Case study

The case study I chose to explore is one with
respect to God.

I believe this has lessons engrained and in waiting for discovery.

Jesus was asked a lot of questions.

He asked even more. There is beauty in questions!

# Job

[For more info](https://www.academia.edu/41661888/Count_of_Questions_in_the_Book_of_Job)

This book is the question feast.

Questions being thrown to everyone. Intentions scattered.

### Question Breakdown by Speaker

<Quote>
Job: ~122 to 139 questions (expressing deep lament, searching for justice, and questioning why he was born or why the righteous suffer).

God: ~61 to 88 questions (responding out of the whirlwind in chapters 38–41 to humble Job with the vast realities of creation).

Eliphaz: ~30 to 39 questions (the first and most prominent friend, using traditional retribution theology).

Bildad: ~9 to 17 questions (the second friend, focusing sternly on divine justice and the fate of the wicked).

Zophar: ~1 to 17 questions (the third friend, who speaks more harshly and interrogates Job's innocence).

Elihu: ~9 to 22 questions (the younger fourth man who speaks in chapters 32–37 before God appears).

Job’s Wife: 1 question (found in Job 2:9: "Do you still hold fast your integrity? Curse God and die!").

Satan: 2 questions (in the prologue, challenging God regarding the nature of Job’s piety).

</Quote>
#### Observations

Job was deeply troubled. He really wanted to know why.
A lot is asked!

He begins by questioning his survival at birth!
He believes it would have been better if he perished during birth!

God answered by questions(classic). He shows Job how little he knows.
Job would really appreciate answers!

He challenges Job's position in the questions he asks.
He deems him without knowledge and one who darkens
counsel by words!

The friends really wanted to figure out what bad things Job did
to suffer such retributions. They believe, rightly, that the
wicked suffer for their deeds and the "good" enjoy life, free of
God's suffering(the big ones).

Job's wife wanted Job out of his suffering by all means, even by cursing God.

Satan, trouble maker as always, brings suffering to Job.
He presents himself to God and when asked about Job he says
Job is only with God because of God's goodness to Job.
Challenges God. Permission granted. Job suffers. 2 round.
First his property and children, then his own body.

Job to his friends is packed. He believes no man can be righteous before God
(including them that seek stains in his life).

# The big guys

### Inquisitive questions

These are child-like, genuine, out of heart questions that seek clarity,
wisdom and understanding.
It is the careful treading into the land unknown, a journey and pursuit deemed worthy!

### Questioning questions

These are the arrogance led, pride filled and trap oriented questions.
The Pharisees were experts at this. Adam the pioneer.(No judgement on them from me)

These undoubtedly deserve no answers!
